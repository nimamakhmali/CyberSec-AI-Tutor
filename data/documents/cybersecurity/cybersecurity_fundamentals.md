# Cybersecurity Fundamentals

## Introduction to Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting systems, networks, and programs from digital attacks. These cyberattacks are usually aimed at accessing, changing, or destroying sensitive information; extorting money from users; or interrupting normal business processes.

## The CIA Triad

The foundation of cybersecurity is built on three core principles known as the CIA Triad:

### Confidentiality
Ensuring that information is accessible only to those authorized to have access. This involves encryption, access controls, and authentication mechanisms.

### Integrity
Maintaining the accuracy and completeness of data over its entire lifecycle. This includes protection against unauthorized modification or deletion.

### Availability
Ensuring that authorized users have access to information and associated assets when needed. This involves redundancy, backup systems, and disaster recovery planning.

## Common Threat Types

### Malware
Malicious software designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to computer systems. Types include:
- **Viruses**: Self-replicating code that attaches to clean files
- **Worms**: Self-replicating malware that spreads across networks
- **Trojans**: Malware disguised as legitimate software
- **Ransomware**: Encrypts files and demands payment for decryption
- **Spyware**: Secretly monitors user activity

### Social Engineering
Psychological manipulation of people into performing actions or divulging confidential information:
- **Phishing**: Fraudulent emails/messages appearing to be from trusted sources
- **Spear Phishing**: Targeted phishing attacks
- **Pretexting**: Creating a fabricated scenario to obtain information
- **Baiting**: Offering something enticing to lure victims

### Network Attacks
- **Man-in-the-Middle (MitM)**: Intercepting communications between two parties
- **Denial of Service (DoS/DDoS)**: Overwhelming a system with traffic
- **DNS Spoofing**: Redirecting traffic to malicious sites
- **ARP Poisoning**: Associating attacker's MAC with victim's IP

## Defense in Depth

A layered security approach where multiple defensive mechanisms protect assets:
1. **Physical Security**: Locks, cameras, access cards
2. **Perimeter Security**: Firewalls, intrusion detection systems
3. **Network Security**: Segmentation, VPNs, monitoring
4. **Endpoint Security**: Antivirus, EDR, patch management
5. **Application Security**: Secure coding, WAF, testing
6. **Data Security**: Encryption, DLP, classification
7. **Identity & Access**: MFA, least privilege, PAM

## Security Frameworks

- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework**: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover
- **ISO 27001**: Information security management standard
- **CIS Controls**: Prioritized cybersecurity best practices
- **MITRE ATT&CK**: Knowledge base of adversary tactics and techniques
